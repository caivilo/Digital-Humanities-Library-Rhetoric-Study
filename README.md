#Communicating Digital Humanities Work in Academic Libraries

#A Multi-Institution Rhetorical Analysis

Researcher: Olivia Coleman
Status: Ongoing independent research
Research areas: Digital Humanities, Academic Librarianship, Scholarly Communication, Qualitative Research, Research Data Management

#About the Project

This independent research project examines how academic libraries communicate their digital humanities services, professional expertise, and collaborative roles through public-facing institutional materials.

Using a qualitative multiple-case study approach, the research focuses on nine U.S. academic libraries and investigates how websites, LibGuides, mission statements, and digital scholarship documentation shape perceptions of librarian expertise, institutional identity, and interdisciplinary collaboration.

#Research Questions

How do academic libraries construct and communicate their digital humanities identities through public-facing materials?

How do institutional communications represent librarians' roles, expertise, and contributions to digital humanities research?

What rhetorical patterns emerge across institutions with different organizational structures and digital humanities infrastructures?

#Methodology

The project draws on directed qualitative content analysis, rhetorical analysis, and comparative case study methods.

The research design includes:

Purposive sampling of nine U.S. academic libraries.

Collection and archival of publicly accessible institutional webpages and LibGuides.

Development of a qualitative codebook examining institutional identity, labor visibility, collaboration, and communication strategies.

Documentation of research methods, source selection, and analytical decisions.

Planned cross-case comparison of rhetorical patterns across institutions.

#Research Documentation

The repository supports the organization and documentation of the project's research materials, including archived sources, methodological documentation, and research design materials.

A comprehensive research proposal outlines the theoretical framework, sampling strategy, coding methodology, research ethics, and planned analysis.

Current Status

Research design and initial source archiving are underway. A preliminary coding framework has been developed, and further analysis is ongoing.

Potential future research includes semi-structured interviews with digital humanities librarians and practitioners, subject to appropriate ethical review.
